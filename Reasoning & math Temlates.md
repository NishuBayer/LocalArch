# A. Arithmetical and Numerical Ability Templates

## 1. Simplification / BODMAS

### Template 1: Mixed operations
> Simplify:  
> [Number 1] + [Number 2] × [Number 3] − [Number 4] ÷ [Number 5]

**Method:** Follow BODMAS:
1. Brackets
2. Orders/powers
3. Division and multiplication
4. Addition and subtraction

---

### Template 2: Surds
> Simplify:  
> #$#\sqrt{[A]}+\sqrt{[B]}-\sqrt{[C]}#$#

**Method:**  
- Simplify individual square roots.
- Use #$#\sqrt{a^2b}=a\sqrt{b}#$#.
- Combine only like surds.

---

### Template 3: Exponents
> Find the value of:  
> #$#[[a]^{m}\times[a]^{n}]\div[a]^{p}#$#

**Method:**  
#$#a^m \times a^n=a^{m+n}#$#  
#$#a^m\div a^n=a^{m-n}#$#

## 2. HCF, LCM and Number System

### Template 1: Find the other number
> The HCF of two numbers is [HCF] and their LCM is [LCM]. If one number is [A], find the other number.

**Formula:**  
#$#\text{First Number}\times\text{Second Number}=\text{HCF}\times\text{LCM}#$#

Therefore:

#$#\text{Other Number}=\frac{\text{HCF}\times\text{LCM}}{\text{Given Number}}#$#

---

### Template 2: Perfect square multiplier
> By what least number should [N] be multiplied to make it a perfect square?

**Method:**
1. Prime-factorise [N].
2. Find factors with odd powers.
3. Multiply by the missing prime factors.

---

### Template 3: Number divisible by two values
> If a number is divisible by both [A] and [B], it must also be divisible by which of the following?

**Method:** Find the LCM.

#$#\text{Required divisor}=\text{LCM}([A],[B])#$#

---

### Template 4: Prime numbers in interval
> How many prime numbers are there between [A] and [B]?

**Method:** Check each number in the interval for divisibility up to its square root.

## 3. Ratio and Proportion

### Template 1: Ratio changes after adding a number
> Two numbers are in the ratio [a] : [b]. If [c] is added to each number, the ratio becomes [d] : [e]. Find the larger number.

**Method:** Assume numbers are #$#ax#$# and #$#bx#$#.

Use:

#$#\frac{ax+c}{bx+c}=\frac{d}{e}#$#

---

### Template 2: Divide an amount in a ratio
> [Amount] is divided among [Person 1], [Person 2], and [Person 3] in the ratio [a] : [b] : [c]. Find the share of [Person].

**Formula:**

#$#\text{Share}=\frac{\text{Individual ratio}}{\text{Sum of ratios}}\times\text{Total amount}#$#

---

### Template 3: Ratio after equal addition
> [Amount] is divided in the ratio [a] : [b] : [c]. If [x] is added to each share, find the new ratio.

**Method:**
1. Find original shares.
2. Add [x] to each.
3. Simplify the new ratio.

---

### Template 4: Mean proportional
> If the mean proportional between [A] and [B] is [M], find [A] / [B].

**Formula:**

#$#M^2=A\times B#$#

---

### Template 5: Third proportional
> If [A], [B], and [x] are in continued proportion, find [x].

**Formula:**

#$#A:B=B:x#$#

Therefore:

#$#x=\frac{B^2}{A}#$#

## 4. Percentage

### Template 1: Percentage increase/decrease
> A number is reduced by [x]%, becoming [A]. By what percentage should it be increased to become [B]?

**Method:**
1. Find original number:
   #$#\text{Original}=\frac{A\times100}{100-x}#$#
2. Find percentage increase:
   #$#\frac{B-A}{A}\times100#$#  
   or as asked, from the appropriate base value.

---

### Template 2: Successive percentage change
> The price of an item is increased successively by [a]%, [b]%, and [c]%, then reduced by [d]%. If final price is ₹[F], find the original price.

**Formula:**

#$#F=P\left(1+\frac{a}{100}\right)\left(1+\frac{b}{100}\right)\left(1+\frac{c}{100}\right)\left(1-\frac{d}{100}\right)#$#

---

### Template 3: Income, expenditure and savings
> A person’s monthly expenditure is ₹[E]. Savings are [s]% of expenditure. Income increases by [i]% and savings increase by [x]%. Find the percentage or numerical change in expenditure.

**Method:**
1. Savings:
   #$#S=\frac{s}{100}\times E#$#
2. Original income:
   #$#I=E+S#$#
3. Calculate new income and new savings.
4. New expenditure:
   #$#E'=I'-S'#$#

---

## 5. Profit, Loss, Marked Price and Discount

### Template 1: Marked price and discount
> A shopkeeper marks an article [x]% above cost price and gives a discount of [y]%. If profit is ₹[P], find the marked price.

**Method:** Let cost price be #$#C#$#.

#$#MP=C\left(1+\frac{x}{100}\right)#$#

#$#SP=MP\left(1-\frac{y}{100}\right)#$#

#$#SP-C=P#$#

---

### Template 2: Successive discounts
> The difference between selling prices after a single discount of [a]% and successive discounts of [b]% and [c]% is ₹[D]. Find marked price.

**Formula:**

Single-discount selling price:

#$#SP_1=MP\left(1-\frac{a}{100}\right)#$#

Successive-discount selling price:

#$#SP_2=MP\left(1-\frac{b}{100}\right)\left(1-\frac{c}{100}\right)#$#

Then:

#$#SP_1-SP_2=D#$#

---

### Template 3: Free articles plus discount
> A trader gives [d]% discount and offers [f] free articles on purchase of [n] articles. He gains [p]%. Find by what percentage the marked price exceeds cost price.

**Method:**
1. Customer receives #$#n+f#$# articles.
2. Customer pays only for #$#n#$# articles.
3. Use effective selling price per article.
4. Apply profit percentage.

---

### Template 4: Mixture profit/loss
> A person buys [x] kg of [item] at ₹[a]/kg and [y] kg at ₹[b]/kg. The mixture is sold at ₹[s]/kg. Find profit/loss.

**Formula:**

#$#CP=(x\times a)+(y\times b)#$#

#$#SP=(x+y)\times s#$#

#$#\text{Profit/Loss}=SP-CP#$#

---

## 6. Simple Interest and Compound Interest

### Template 1: Split investment at two rates
> [Person] invests ₹[P] partly at [r1]% p.a. and partly at [r2]% p.a. For [t] years, both investments earn equal interest. Find the amount invested at [r1]%.

**Method:** If investments are #$#x#$# and #$#P-x#$#:

#$#\frac{x\times r_1\times t}{100}=\frac{(P-x)\times r_2\times t}{100}#$#

---

### Template 2: Compound amount with changing rates
> ₹[P] is invested for [n] years at [r1]% in the first year, [r2]% in the second year, and so on. Find the final amount.

**Formula:**

#$#A=P\left(1+\frac{r_1}{100}\right)\left(1+\frac{r_2}{100}\right)\cdots#$#

---

### Template 3: Compound interest with annual instalments
> A person borrows ₹[P] at [r]% compound interest p.a. and pays ₹[I] at the end of each year. Find amount due after [n] instalments.

**Method for each year:**

#$#\text{New balance}=(\text{Old balance})\left(1+\frac{r}{100}\right)-I#$#

Repeat for every year.

---

### Template 4: Rate from consecutive compound amounts
> The compound amounts after [n] and [n+1] years are ₹[A] and ₹[B]. Find annual rate of interest.

**Formula:**

#$#B=A\left(1+\frac{r}{100}\right)#$#

Therefore:

#$#r=\left(\frac{B}{A}-1\right)\times100#$#

---

## 7. Average

### Template 1: Average after adding members
> The average age of [n] students is [A]. When [m] new students join, the average becomes [B]. Find the average age of the new students.

**Formula:**

#$#\text{Average of new students}=\frac{(n+m)B-nA}{m}#$#

---

### Template 2: Ratio-based prices
> The average price of [n] items is ₹[A]. Their prices are in the ratio [a] : [b] : [c]. Find the price of the costliest item.

**Method:**
1. Total:
   #$#T=n\times A#$#
2. One ratio unit:
   #$#k=\frac{T}{a+b+c}#$#
3. Costliest:
   #$#\text{Largest ratio}\times k#$#

---

### Template 3: Average after multiplying every observation
> The average of [n] values is [A]. If every value is multiplied by [k], find the new average.

**Formula:**

#$#\text{New average}=k\times A#$#

---

## 8. Partnership

### Template 1: Capital changes during the year
> [A] and [B] invest in the ratio [a] : [b]. After [m] months, [A] changes capital by [x]% and [B] changes capital by [y]%. If [B] receives ₹[P] as profit, find [A]’s profit.

**Formula:** Profit ratio is proportional to:

#$#\text{Capital}\times\text{Time}#$#

Create a capital-month table:

| Partner | Initial capital × months | Revised capital × months |
|---|---:|---:|
| A | [Capital A] × [months] | [New capital A] × [remaining months] |
| B | [Capital B] × [months] | [New capital B] × [remaining months] |

## 9. Time and Work

### Template 1: Two persons and a third helper
> [A] completes work in [a] days and [B] in [b] days. With [C], they complete the work in [t] days. Find time taken by [C] alone.

**Formula:**

#$#\frac{1}{C}=\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{b}#$#

---

### Template 2: Person takes fewer days
> [A] takes [d] days less than [B] to complete a work. Together they finish in [t] days. Find time taken by [A] or [B].

**Method:** Let B take #$#x#$# days; A takes #$#x-d#$# days.

#$#\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{x-d}=\frac{1}{t}#$#

---

### Template 3: Three pairs of workers
> [A], [B], and [C] together complete work in [t] days. [A+B], [B+C], and [C+A] take [x], [y], and [z] days. Find time taken by [A]/[B]/[C].

**Method:** Use work rates:

#$#A+B=\frac{1}{x},\quad B+C=\frac{1}{y},\quad C+A=\frac{1}{z}#$#

Then:

#$#A+B+C=\frac{1}{t}#$#

Solve by subtraction.

---

## 10. Pipes and Cisterns

### Template 1: Two filling pipes
> One pipe fills a tank [k] times as fast as another. Together they fill the tank in [t] minutes. Find time taken by slower pipe.

**Method:** Let slower pipe’s rate be #$#x#$#.

Faster pipe rate:

#$#kx#$#

Together:

#$#(k+1)x=\frac{1}{t}#$#

---

### Template 2: Filling and waste pipe
> Pipe A fills a tank in [a] hours, Pipe B fills it in [b] hours, and a waste pipe empties it in [c] hours. Find time taken when all are open.

**Formula:**

#$#\text{Net rate}=\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}-\frac{1}{c}#$#

Then:

#$#\text{Time}=\frac{1}{\text{Net rate}}#$#

---

### Template 3: Volume-rate filling
> [A] pours [x] litres every [a] minutes and [B] pours [y] litres every [b] minutes. How long will they take to fill [V] litres?

**Formula:**

#$#\text{Combined rate}=\frac{x}{a}+\frac{y}{b}#$#

#$#\text{Time}=\frac{V}{\text{Combined rate}}#$#

---

## 11. Time, Speed and Distance

### Template 1: Train crossing pole and platform
> A train crosses a pole in [t1] seconds and a platform of length [L] metres in [t2] seconds. Find train length and speed.

**Formula:**

#$#L_{\text{train}}=v\times t_1#$#

#$#L_{\text{train}}+L=v\times t_2#$#

Subtract:

#$#L=v(t_2-t_1)#$#

---

### Template 2: Relative speed
> [A] and [B] travel from [Point A] to [Point B] at [s1] km/h and [s2] km/h. [B] arrives [t] minutes earlier. Find distance.

**Formula:**

#$#\frac{D}{s_1}-\frac{D}{s_2}=\frac{t}{60}#$#

---

### Template 3: Boat and stream
> Speed of boat in still water is [b] km/h and speed of stream is [s] km/h. Find time to travel [D1] km upstream and [D2] km downstream.

**Formula:**

#$#\text{Upstream speed}=b-s#$#

#$#\text{Downstream speed}=b+s#$#

#$#\text{Total time}=\frac{D_1}{b-s}+\frac{D_2}{b+s}#$#

---

### Template 4: Required speed for remaining journey
> A person covers [fraction/part] of a journey in [t1] minutes. To cover the remaining journey in [t2] minutes, what speed is required?

**Method:**
1. Find total distance from first part.
2. Find remaining distance.
3. Use:
   #$#v=\frac{d}{t}#$#

---

## 12. Mensuration

### Template 1: Cylinder tank emptied at a rate
> A cylindrical tank of diameter [d] m is emptied at [r] cubic metres per second. Find time to make it [fraction] empty.

**Formula:**

#$#V=\pi r^2h#$#

Then:

#$#t=\frac{\text{Required volume}}{\text{Discharge rate}}#$#

---

### Template 2: Cubical tank and tiles
> A cubical tank of edge [a] m is tiled externally except its base using square tiles of side [b] cm. Cost is ₹[C] per dozen. Find total cost.

**Method:**
1. Surface area to cover:
   #$#5a^2#$# if the tank has a lid and base is excluded.
2. Tile area:
   #$#b^2#$# after converting units.
3. Tiles needed:
   #$#\frac{\text{Area to cover}}{\text{Tile area}}#$#
4. Cost:
   #$#\frac{\text{Tiles}}{12}\times C#$#

---

### Template 3: Cone curved surface area
> Curved surface area of a cone is [CSA] cm² and diameter is [d] cm. Find slant height.

**Formula:**

#$#CSA=\pi rl#$#

Therefore:

#$#l=\frac{CSA}{\pi r}#$#

---

### Template 4: Cone cut parallel to base
> A cone of height [h] and radius [r] is cut at [fraction] of its height parallel to base. Find ratio of original cone CSA to frustum CSA.

**Method:**
1. Use similarity:
   #$#\frac{r_1}{r}=\frac{h_1}{h}#$#
2. Find slant heights.
3. Frustum CSA:
   #$#\pi(R+r)l#$#

---

### Template 5: Largest circle inside rectangle
> Find circumference of the largest circle that can be inscribed in a rectangle of dimensions [l] m and [b] m.

**Method:**
- Diameter equals smaller side.

#$#d=\min(l,b)#$#

#$#C=\pi d#$#

---

# B. General Intelligence and Reasoning Templates

## 1. Coding-Decoding

### Template 1: Letter-to-number coding
> In a certain code language, “[WORD 1]” is coded as “[NUMBER CODE 1]” and “[WORD 2]” is coded as “[NUMBER CODE 2]”. What is the code for “[LETTER / WORD]”?

**Method:**
1. Match common letters in both words.
2. Match common digits in both codes.
3. Identify the letter-digit relationship.
4. Apply to asked letter.

---

### Template 2: Alphabet shift coding
> “[WORD / LETTER CLUSTER]” is related to “[CODE]” in a certain way. Following the same logic, how is “[NEW WORD / CLUSTER]” related?

**Common rules to test:**
- Add/subtract a fixed alphabet position.
- Reverse alphabet positions.
- Alternate #$#+n#$# and #$#-n#$#.
- Shift first/second/third letters by different values.
- Convert letters based on position.

---

### Template 3: Vowel-consonant coding
> In each word, every vowel is changed to the next letter and every consonant to the preceding letter. In how many resulting clusters does [condition] occur?

**Method:**
- Rewrite each word letter-by-letter.
- Apply separate vowel and consonant rule.
- Count only after complete conversion.

## 2. Number Series

### Template 1: Alternating difference series
> Find the missing term:  
> [A], [B], [C], [D], [E], ?

**Method:**
Check:
- constant differences,
- alternating differences,
- multiplication/division,
- squares/cubes,
- prime numbers,
- combination of two sequences.

Example structure:

#$#A\;(-x)\;B\;(-y)\;C\;(-x)\;D\;(-y)\;E#$#

---

### Template 2: Number relation without digit splitting
> [A] is related to [B] following a certain logic. [C] is related to [D] using the same logic. To which option is [E] related?

**Method:**
Try operations on the full number only:

#$#B=A\div k#$#  
#$#B=A\times k#$#  
#$#B=A\pm k#$#  
#$#B=A\pm A/k#$#

Do **not** split digits when instruction forbids it.

---

### Template 3: Number-set relation
> In each set, operations on the first two numbers produce the third:  
> ([a], [b], [c])  
> ([d], [e], [f])  
> Find the matching set.

**Method:**
Test:

#$#c=a\times b#$#  
#$#c=a+b#$#  
#$#c=a^2\pm b#$#  
#$#c=a(b\pm1)#$#  
#$#c=a\times(b\pm k)#$#

## 9. Syllogism / Statement-Conclusion

### Template 1: All / some / no statements

> 1. All [A] are [B].

> 3. No [C] is [D].

# How to Use These Templates

For every template, create at least **10 self-practice variants** by changing only:

```text
Names
Numbers
Dates
Objects
Units
Directions
Options
Context
```

For example, instead of memorising a specific pipe question, generate:

> Pipe A fills in **[a] hours**, Pipe B fills in **[b] hours**, and Pipe C empties in **[c] hours**. How long will all three take together?

Then vary:

- #$#a=8,\;b=12,\;c=24#$#
- #$#a=10,\;b=15,\;c=30#$#
- #$#a=6,\;b=9,\;c=18#$#

The **template remains identical**, but the arithmetic changes.

The same principle applies to:

- coding,
- direction,
- blood relation,
- seating,
- series,
- profit-loss,
- work,
- mensuration,
- DI.
